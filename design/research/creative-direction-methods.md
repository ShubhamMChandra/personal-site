# How people find a mood, a theme or a brand

Research notes for the personal-site redesign. 14 October 2026.

Five research sweeps ran in parallel, one per field. Each read primary sources where it could:
the practitioners' own writing, talks, papers and books. This file is reference. The short
version and the proposed process come first. The notes on each method, with sources, follow.

---

## The short version

- **Take the idea from the real thing. Don't apply a theme.** Four of the five fields say this.
  Aesop builds each store from its site. Alexander tells you to fill rooms with things from your
  own life. Gassner designed one object for Blade Runner 2049 and made everything else match it.
  The personal-site writers took colour from a notebook or a studio wall. The bookshelf was a
  theme applied from outside. So was the list of 25 ideas.
- **Ask "which is more like you?", not "which do you like?"** This is Christopher Alexander's
  test. Liking pulls in status, brands and associations. "More like you" asks something closer
  to "is it me."
- **The person whose taste it is supplies the material and names the differences.** In round
  one the facilitator picked all 72 images and named the themes. So even the "confident" themes
  are partly the facilitator's words.
- **Pick first. Then ask "which part?", not "why?"** Asking people to explain why they like
  something can change what they pick (Wilson et al., 1993). Miyazaki praised parts of a
  sketch, and his art director combined the praised parts.
- **Keep early work crude, plentiful and cheap to throw away.** Pixar painted 500 to 600 small
  colour frames for Inside Out and kept about 200. Finished-looking work gets judged as finished.
- **Write the refusals next to the wants.** Chimero's flat "no" lines, anti-tone words, Koster's
  yes and no tables.
- **Decide what kind of thing it is, and how it changes over time, before how it looks.**
  Chimero places a site between app and document. Schwulst's metaphors (a plant, a garden, a
  thrown rock) are about tempo and upkeep, not imagery.

## What wam.team and Unlock Intelligence already do

Neither site is a place or a room. Each is built on one visual idea taken from what the
business does.

- **wam.team.** The busywork is a pile of sticky notes. The hero lets you drag them into a tray
  and hand them off. The highlighter in the logo and the footer line about highlighters carry
  the same idea to the end of the page. Warm light ground, light and witty.
- **Unlock Intelligence.** One graphic element, a line. It starts as a dashed handoff between
  departments. It breaks into separate strokes for "one chat at a time." It comes back together
  as a stream, with amber marking the step an agent runs. Dark ground.

What they share: one motif, taken from the subject, carried through every section, and it does
something when you touch it or scroll. That is the same move the research recommends. For a
personal site, the subject is the person.

## Proposed process

1. **Gather.** Over a few days, you send things from your own life that feel like it, and a few
   that feel wrong: places, objects, moments, work you've made. Words or photos. No brief. Your
   Astor Place memory is exactly this kind of thing.
   *From: cultural probes and photo elicitation; ZMET; Aesop; Alexander's pattern 253.*
2. **Name.** Your things are shown three at a time. You say which one is different and what its
   opposite would be. Six or so rounds. Your words become the dimensions. Add a list of refusals.
   *From: repertory grid; Chimero; Koster's yes and no tables.*
3. **Sketch.** Many small rough frames, not pages. Some are motifs like the WAM notes, some are
   places, some are only type and colour. You answer "which is more like you?" and point at the
   part that did it. To learn what causes a reaction, change one thing at a time.
   *From: Alexander; Miyazaki and Oga; Eggleston; style tiles; element collages; Kansei
   engineering.*
4. **Compress and prove.** A word or short phrase, a short yes and no list, and a decision on how
   the site changes over time. Then build one piece fully and check it on your phone: "does this
   look like me?"
   *From: Gassner; Koster; Clearleft; Schwulst and Sloan; Pixar's Coco bridge test; Gaver.*

The feeling paragraph from today stays as a running draft. It is not a gate.

## Evidence quality

Most of this is practitioner practice, not validated research. Specific cautions:

- Alexander's claim that 70 to 80% of people agree on "more like myself" is his own figure. No
  independent replication was found. A 2025 paper is suggestive, but it primed participants first.
- Prospect-refuge theory is contested. Support for "prospect" is stronger than for "refuge."
- Microsoft's reaction cards have no evidence that they measure desirability. Use them only to
  start a conversation.
- Kansei engineering needs many raters for statistics. With one person it gives hints, not proof.
- Most of the personal-site accounts are single cases.

## Method notes, by field

### Brand studios and web design

- **Style tiles** (Samantha Warren, 2012). Rate word pairs, each anchored to an example site. Then
  build two or three tiles of type, colour and interface pieces, with no layout. Each tile pushes
  a different direction. https://alistapart.com/article/style-tiles-and-how-they-work
- **Element collages** (Dan Mall, 2012; Cloud Four, 2016). Loose pieces at any stage of finish,
  named so nobody mistakes them for a design. Cloud Four had each designer build one alone, ran a
  survey, then let them borrow each other's best parts.
  https://cloudfour.com/thinks/element-collages-are-fun/ (Mall's original:
  https://web.archive.org/web/2013/http://danielmall.com/articles/rif-element-collages/)
- **Stylescapes, hot / medium / mild** (The Futur). Three boards at three intensities of one
  reading of the brief. Weakness: they can't test a wrong direction.
  https://thefutur.com/content/what-are-stylescapes
- **Distinct routes and one idea** (Koto, 2016; Wolff Olins). Koto showed four routes, each built
  from one core thought plus layers, differing in kind. Wolff Olins looks for the idea where
  "what's special about you" meets "what the world needs," and tests it on an outsider.
  https://www.itsnicethat.com/features/lecture-in-progress-parts-of-the-process-koto-041016 ,
  https://wolffolins.com/news/inside-wolff-olins-sammy-page-on-strategy
- **Reaction cards and tone sliders** (Microsoft, 2002; Nielsen Norman Group, 2016). Pick five
  words from 118, about 40% negative, then explain each. Place a voice on four spectra and add
  anti-tone words. https://measuringu.com/?p=578 ,
  https://www.nngroup.com/articles/tone-of-voice-dimensions/
- **Designer as their own client** (Frank Chimero, 2020; Lea Alcantara, 2009). Chimero placed his
  site between app and document, wrote four short wants with flat refusals, kept the live site
  plain, specified the system on paper, and cut pages that felt self-involved. Alcantara wrote a
  brief first and asked two trusted friends to check it.
  https://web.archive.org/web/2021/https://frankchimero.com/blog/2020/wants-and-needs/ ,
  https://alistapart.com/article/redesigning-your-own-site/
- **Design principles** (Clearleft) and **structured moodboards** (Viget, 2009).
  https://principles.design/examples/clearleft-principles-for-design-principles ,
  https://www.viget.com/articles/perspectives-on-mood-boards/

### Film, animation and games

- **One word, then a pattern language** (Dennis Gassner, Blade Runner 2049). The director gave one
  word. Gassner designed the first object the lead uses, then made every later object match it.
  https://theasc.com/articles/blade-runner-2049-designing-the-future
- **Image book** (Todd Haynes). A book of collected images for each film, handed to department
  heads at the start. https://library.brown.edu/create/libnews/exhibit-haynes-1995-2017/
  (secondhand)
- **Image boards** (Hayao Miyazaki). Many small, fast pencil sketches with a colour wash, made
  before the story is fixed and pinned on a wall. Kazuo Oga combined the parts Miyazaki praised.
  https://animationobsessive.substack.com/p/the-image-boards-of-hayao-miyazaki ,
  https://animationobsessive.substack.com/p/defining-the-world-of-my-neighbor
- **Colour script** (Pixar, Ralph Eggleston). One small painting per story beat, showing colour
  and light. Redone when the story shifts. https://www.awn.com/animationworld/ralph-eggleston-talks-inside-out
- **Mood boards, then an illustration bible** (Patrice Vermette, Dune). Long exploration, then a
  hard lock on about 125 illustrations.
  https://www.wix.com/studio/blog/dune-production-designer-patrice-vermette
- **Experience pillars and yes / no tables** (MDA framework; Raph Koster). Name the feelings
  first, write a few pillars, list what it is and what it is not.
  https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~hunicke/MDA.pdf , https://www.raphkoster.com/?p=46260
- **Stylization with a purpose** (Valve, Team Fortress 2, GDC 2008). State why the look exists,
  rank what the viewer reads first, solve shapes in silhouette.
  https://cdn.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2008/GDC2008_StylizationWithAPurpose_TF2.pdf

### Architecture, interiors and lighting

- **"Which is more like your own self?"** (Christopher Alexander). Compare two things on that
  question, then name the cause with his fifteen properties.
  https://jackcheng.com/sunday/386-mirrors-of-the-self/ ,
  https://www.patternlanguage.com/archive/ieee.html
- **Pattern 253, things from your life** and **pattern 127, intimacy gradient** (A Pattern
  Language, 1977). https://urbigenous.net/a-pattern-language/pattern-253.html ,
  https://urbigenous.net/a-pattern-language/pattern-127.html
- **Nine points of atmosphere** (Peter Zumthor, 2006): body, materials, sound, temperature,
  surrounding objects, composure and seduction, inside and outside, intimacy, light on things.
  Useful as questions to put to any image.
  https://books.google.com/books/about/Atmospheres.html?id=sZFrDJAyOxMC
- **Aesop store design** (Denise Neri). Read the site, pick one idea, choose materials, strip out
  anything too literal.
  https://www.optionstheedge.com/topic/haven/aesop-store-design-speaks-sensitively-local-surroundings-honouring-neighbourhoods-and
- **Oku, miegakure, ma** (Maki, Nitschke, Isozaki). Depth reached in layers. Part of the scene is
  hidden from any one viewpoint, so you have to move. Secondary sources only.
  https://kyotojournal.org/culture-arts/ma-place-space-void/
- **Prospect, refuge, mystery** (Appleton, Hildebrand, the Kaplans). Seeing out while sheltered,
  plus the promise that going further shows more. Contested.
  https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/blockout/massing/prospect-refuge
- **Three kinds of light** (Richard Kelly, 1952): focal glow, ambient luminescence, play of
  brilliants. No published web use found. https://richardkellygrant.org/who-is-richard-kelly/
- **The Image of the City** (Kevin Lynch, 1960). Paths, edges, districts, nodes, landmarks. Tests
  structure, not mood.
  https://cus.ubt-uni.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Kevin-Lynch-The-Image-of-the-City.pdf

### Drawing out someone's taste

- **Repertory grid** (George Kelly, 1955). Show three things. Ask which is different and what the
  opposite would be, in the person's words. Rate everything on that scale. Quality drops after
  about eight triads.
  https://jyx.jyu.fi/bitstream/handle/123456789/25716/1/HumTech_2010_v06_n02_p_250-268.pdf
- **Laddering** (Reynolds and Gutman). Ask why a feature matters, three or four times. Then go
  back down: "what would you see on a page?" https://arxiv.org/pdf/1607.07796
- **Semantic differential into Kansei engineering** (Osgood; Nagamachi). Rate a designed set of
  samples on feeling scales, then see which design properties move which feelings.
  https://ep.liu.se/ecp/146/003/ecp18146003.pdf
- **Cultural probes and photo elicitation** (Gaver, Dunne and Pacenti, 1999). The person collects
  material over days. Success means they recognise themselves in what comes back.
  https://cfacaa.human.cornell.edu/dea.arl/linked%20docs/Cultural%20Probes_Gaver.pdf
- **ZMET** (Gerald Zaltman). A week of collecting images, a story for each, the image you wanted
  but couldn't find, then sorting and triads. The full version risks feeling like a test.
  https://eprints.bournemouth.ac.uk/35323/
- **Pairwise choice with one change at a time** (O'Donovan, 2014; Koyama, 2017 and 2020).
  https://www.dgp.toronto.edu/~donovan/font/fontSelection.pdf
- **Caution.** Wilson et al. (1993): people who analysed their reasons for liking posters chose
  differently and were less satisfied weeks later. Abstract only.

### Personal websites

- **Laurel Schwulst** (2018). A site can be a room, a shelf, a plant, a garden, a puddle or a
  thrown rock. Each implies a tempo and how much upkeep it needs. Her prompt: what feeling should
  it give you?
  https://thecreativeindependent.com/essays/laurel-schwulst-my-website-is-a-shifting-house-next-to-a-river-of-knowledge-what-could-yours-be/
- **Robin Sloan** (2020). Build for people you can name, not a market.
  https://www.robinsloan.com/notes/home-cooked-app/
- **The garden lineage** (Mark Bernstein, 1998; Mike Caulfield, 2015; Maggie Appleton). Indirect
  paths, marked edges, organised by relationship instead of date, rough edges shown.
  http://www.eastgate.com/garden/Seven_Lessons.html , https://maggieappleton.com/garden-history
- **Frank Chimero, The Web's Grain** (2015). The web is fluid, vertical and edgeless. Screens are
  neutral about looks and built for change. https://frankchimero.com/blog/2015/the-webs-grain/
- **Kyle Chayka, AirSpace** (2016) and **Filterworld** (2024). The sameness people react against.
  His remedy is personal taste.
- **IndieWeb principles.** Make what you need, use what you make, have fun.
  https://indieweb.org/principles
- **First-person accounts.** Osvaldas Valutis took his background colour from his notebook
  (https://osvaldas.info/redesign/). Carolyn Yoo dropped a brutalist version that didn't feel like
  home (https://cyoo.substack.com/p/making-my-website-feel-like-home). Lynn Fisher rebuilds her site
  every year (https://lynnandtonic.com). Andy Matuschak writes his notes mostly for himself
  (https://notes.andymatuschak.org/About_these_notes).
