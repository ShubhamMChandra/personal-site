# Glass marks: brief

Settled with him on 15 October 2026, one question at a time. His idea, in his words:

> I really want to combine the like transclucent glass effect with the colored marks over the muted
> grey canvas. What if there were a smattering of marks where you could still like see the
> background shift the way that original did and as you opened up the different tabs theyd
> rearrange in some motif that was similar to the content or like a motif related to it that was
> sstill beautiful?

His favourites so far, which this idea combines: round 1 sketches 3 (coloured film) and 5 (painted
strokes), round 2 sketch 2 (colour arriving under the words), round 3 sketch 3 (glass that
assembles), and the leaves still.

## Settled
1. **At rest**, the first screen shows his name, what he does now, and how to reach him. Nothing
   else is assembled. "it doesnt come together till you let it, and theres some light way of
   engagement that changes what could be shown. it needs some level of interactivity."
2. **The mark** is one hand-made dab, shaped like a brush dab or a leaf, made of coloured glass you
   can see through. Overlaps mix like round 1's film.
3. **The touch** is tapping a word. "i still want there to feel like theres a reward for clicking
   and that its somewhat obvious to click."
4. **Obvious** because each word has a fragment beside it: a few marks already laid into the start
   of its pattern. It looks unfinished, so you want to finish it. Hovering starts it growing.
5. **The pattern** is a laid arrangement that echoes the part, like the leaves ring: a ring, a
   spiral, a line, a weave. Never a picture of a thing.
6. **The shift** is the light. It drifts behind the glass and follows a finger or cursor, and the
   marks' coloured shadows slide across the grey canvas, as in round 1.
7. **Layout**: a part opens under its word, like a drawer. Several can be open at once.
8. **Many open**: each open part claims its share of marks. Open everything and every mark is laid:
   the full picture is the whole page composed. Closing a drawer lets its marks loose again.
9. **Placement**: the pattern forms around the words, with the words in the middle, like the
   leaves round the hole.
10. **Phone**: drawers are short and narrow, a few lines each, so the pattern can go all the way
    round on a phone.
11. **Colour**: each part has its own dye colours. The loose marks are every colour mixed, and
    opening a part pulls out the marks in its colours, so you watch them sort as they gather.
12. **Untouched**: both. Lingering draws loose marks slowly toward the fragments, so they grow a
    little. Only opening a part completes its pattern.

## Assumed for the first mockup (his to overturn)
- Ground: muted warm grey, like plaster, with grain.
- Parts and words are placeholders from the v0 copy: places, cloth, cooking, reading, and his
  past roles. The line about his current work is a placeholder too.
- Shown first as stills: at rest, one part open, everything open.
